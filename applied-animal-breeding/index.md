
# Applied Animal Breeding (ANPR 531)

Programme:	MSc Animal Production
Semester:	II
Course status:	Core
Credit hours:	3 (2+1)
lecturer: Hulunim Gatew Tariku

# 1. Course Description
Applied Animal Breeding covers the genetic principles and practical methods used to improve the productivity, efficiency, and sustainability of farm animals. The course addresses the genetic basis of variation, economically important traits, selection and genetic gain, animal identification and performance recording, environmental adjustment of records, breeding value estimation, selection methods, and mating systems. It also examines inbreeding, inbreeding depression, heterosis, pure breeding, and crossbreeding, with emphasis on the design, implementation, and evaluation of effective breeding programmes under different production systems, including smallholder and community-based systems.

# 2. Course Learning Outcomes
1.	Explain the genetic principles underlying animal improvement, including sources of variation, heritability, and genetic correlations.
2.	Identify and prioritise economically important production, reproduction, health, and fitness traits for a defined livestock production system.
3.	Design an appropriate animal identification and performance-recording system and explain how records should be standardised for fair comparisons.
4.	Calculate and interpret genetic parameters, selection differentials, expected genetic responses, relationship coefficients, and inbreeding coefficients.
5.	Compare approaches to estimating breeding values and explain how individual, pedigree, and relatives’ records contribute to genetic evaluation.
6.	Evaluate mating systems, including pure breeding, outcrossing, and crossbreeding, in relation to genetic improvement, inbreeding, and heterosis.
7.	Develop and justify a breeding programme for a defined production system, including objectives, recording, genetic evaluation, selection, mating, and implementation.
8.	Evaluate an existing breeding intervention and make evidence-based recommendations, including consideration of community-based breeding programmes where appropriate.
   
# 3. Course Content
# Unit 1. Introduction to Applied Animal Breeding
•	Definition, objectives, and scope of animal breeding
•	Natural and artificial selection
•	Genetic and environmental sources of variation
•	Phenotypic, genetic, and environmental relationships
•	Heritability and genetic correlations
•	Variance and standard deviation in animal breeding
•	Selection accuracy, selection intensity, generation interval, and expected genetic gain
# Unit 2. Traits in Farm Animals
•	Classification of traits: fitness, production, product quality, type/conformation, and behaviour
•	Objective and subjective measurement of traits
•	Economically important traits in dairy cattle, beef cattle, sheep, and other relevant livestock
•	Environmental effects on performance and the importance of comparable records
•	Prioritising traits according to breeding objectives and production systems
# Unit 3. Recording and Performance Evaluation
•	Animal identification, pedigree recording, and performance recording
•	Features and formats of effective recording systems
•	Environmental adjustment and standardisation of records
•	Performance evaluation and estimated breeding values (EBVs)
•	Use of repeated records and information from relatives
•	Selection index and BLUP, as covered in the teaching material
•	Interpretation and practical use of genetic evaluation results
# Unit 4. Systems of Mating
•	Random and assortative mating
•	Pure breeding and outcrossing
•	Crossbreeding systems and their objectives
•	Choosing mating systems for defined production goals and environments
# Unit 5. Inbreeding, Inbreeding Depression, and Heterosis
•	Pedigree relationships and inbreeding coefficients
•	Causes and consequences of inbreeding and inbreeding depression
•	Management of inbreeding in breeding populations
•	Definition and genetic basis of heterosis
•	Individual and maternal heterosis
•	Effects of heterosis on production, reproduction, health, and fitness
•	Benefits and limitations of crossbreeding and maintaining genetic diversity
# Unit 6. Designing and Implementing Breeding Programmes
•	Context and constraints of breeding programmes in developing-country production systems
•	Reviewing previous breeding interventions and lessons learned
•	Defining breeding objectives and identifying target traits
•	Designing animal identification, data collection, and genetic evaluation systems
•	Selection, mating, expected genetic gain, and management of inbreeding
•	Implementation, dissemination, monitoring, and evaluation
•	Community-based breeding programmes and stakeholder participation

# 4. Teaching and Learning Methods
•	Interactive lectures: Use short explanations, questions, and worked examples to introduce key concepts.
•	Problem-based learning: Have students calculate selection response, interpret heritability, compare candidates, and calculate relationships or inbreeding coefficients.
•	Practical record analysis: Provide sample livestock performance records for checking, summarising, comparing, and interpreting.
•	Case studies: Review breeding interventions and discuss their objectives, outcomes, limitations, and suitability for local production systems.
•	Group work: Ask groups to define a breeding objective and propose a practical breeding programme for a selected livestock production system.
•	Student presentations: Require students to justify their choice of traits, recording strategy, selection method, and mating system.
